Origins of Trust

From East Masi Street to Kitchens Across the World

In the winter of 1978, amidst the chiming bell towers of the Madurai Meenakshi Amman Temple, Sri S. Jayavel rented a modest 180-square-foot doorway along the historic East Masi Street. His inventory was modest: twenty brass Kuthu Vilakkus crafted by family sthapatis in Swamimalai, heavy bronze Urlis, and pure bell-metal cooking pots.

While commercial markets pivoted to flimsy sheet-press metal and lightweight alloys, Sri Jayavel made a solemn vow: “Never sell a vessel you wouldn’t place in your mother’s temple room.” He travelled on overnight train carriages across Kumbakonam, Nachiarkoil, and Thanjavur, uniting master metal-casters into a protected artisan consortium.

family_restroom An Inheritance Passed Across Generations

Today, grandmothers who bought their wedding Seervarisai (bridal vessels) at Jayavel Stores in the 1980s arrive holding the hands of their daughters and granddaughters. The scent of genuine camphor, bell brass, and warmth remains completely unchanged.

Sacred Metallurgical Mastery

The Art of the Craft: Step by Step

How molten elements transform into sacred blessings through centuries-old Tamil traditions.

Step 01

Madhuchehishtavidhana

Lost-Wax Sculpting

Hand-carved beeswax models coated in river clay from the Cauvery bed, dried under temple sunlight, and burnt away to leave hollow impressions.

architecture Chola-era proportion guidelines
Step 02

Manoeuvred Chiseling

Fine Ornament Engraving

Using micro steel styluses, craftsmen etch temple petaled lotuses, sacred Annapakshi birds, and divine crests with breathtaking millimeter precision.

brush 100% Hand-beaten detailing
Step 03

Eeyam Poosuthal

Traditional Food-Safe Tinning

Internal lining of brass and bronze pots with 99.8% pure silver-grade tin (Velli Eeyam) using charcoal heat and natural sal-ammoniac powder.

local_fire_department Ayurvedic acid resistance
Step 04

Suvarna Kanthi

Sacred Buffing & Quality Sanctum

Tamarind and wood-ash polishing reveals the radiant golden mirror lustre that repels premature tarnish while leaving natural tactile warmth.

“Every morning when we unlock our gates at East Masi Street, we welcome family, not customers.”

Whether you are selecting a modest 6-inch hand-bell for your apartment shrine in London or commissioning a 12-foot brass Kodi Maram for a temple renovation in Coimbatore, my store family and I are honored to serve your devotion.
